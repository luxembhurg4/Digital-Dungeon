Master Plan: My Digital Life — The Dungeon of Tabs
1. Project goal
Build a personal digital-life scrapbook presented as a small, explorable dungeon.

The experience combines:

Eight content rooms containing the original scrapbook.
Seven unique hallway maps connecting those rooms.
One consistent movable knight controlled by a D-pad or keyboard.
Physical doorway navigation: the next content room opens only when the character enters its marked entrance.
The dungeon is a navigation layer—not a difficult game. It should make the scrapbook engaging without making the content frustrating to reach.

Scope boundaries
Do not add combat, health, enemies that chase the player, inventory requirements, puzzles, or locked treasure unless requested later.

The Reel Monster is a thematic character. Its presence does not imply a combat system.

3. Complete journey
The sequence is fixed:

Gate → hallway 1 → Profile Hall → hallway 2 → Analysis Chamber → hallway 3 → Router Quest → hallway 4 → Reel Trap → hallway 5 → Plan Armory → hallway 6 → Infographic Vault → hallway 7 → Exit

How each stage works
The visitor starts on the Gate content screen.
Enter the dungeon opens hallway 1—not Profile Hall directly.
The knight appears at that hallway’s defined spawn.
The visitor moves toward the labeled destination entrance.
Crossing the gold threshold opens the destination content room.
Movement stops while the visitor reads or interacts with that room.
A content-room action such as Continue through the dungeon opens the next hallway at its spawn.
The next room must again be entered through its doorway.
Navigation changes from the original webpage
Remove direct Next room shortcuts.
Remove arrow-key shortcuts that instantly switch content rooms.
Do not let chapter tabs jump ahead to unopened rooms.
Use the chapter list primarily to show current position and explored-room status.
A button may open the hallway. It must not bypass the hallway and open the next room directly.

6. Character and movement
Existing asset dimensions
Tile size: 16 × 16 world pixels
Map size: 384 × 288 world pixels
Knight sprite canvas: 16 × 16
Feet anchor within the sprite: (8,15)
Feet collision rectangle: X 4–12, Y 11–15, relative to the sprite’s top-left corner
A spawn coordinate identifies the tile containing the sprite. Initially place the sprite’s top-left corner at that tile’s top-left corner.

Use the feet region for collision—not the entire helmet and body. This allows the character to appear naturally close to walls.

Inputs
Support:

Up, down, left, and right on the keyboard.
The four matching D-pad buttons.
Press-and-hold movement on the D-pad.
Recommended behavior:

No diagonal movement.
When two directions are held, use the most recently pressed direction.
Clear held input when focus is lost, the pointer is released or cancelled, or a content room opens.
Do not intercept typing or navigation keys while a form control has focus.
Sprite states
For each facing direction, use:

Idle
Walk pose 1
Walk pose 2
While moving, alternate the two walking poses. When movement stops—or a wall prevents movement—return to idle while retaining the facing direction.

Suggested starting values, not existing asset requirements:

Movement speed: approximately 64 world pixels per second
Walking animation: approximately 8 pose changes per second
Tune these after testing. Speed should not depend on the device’s frame rate.

7. Doorway behavior and return navigation
Forward entrance
For every destination:

Detect the character’s feet entering either gold threshold cell.
Activate the destination once.
Stop movement and clear held controls.
Open the matching content room.
Mark that room explored.
Move reading focus to the room heading.
The open doorway artwork above the threshold is decorative. Do not create a second trigger there or require the player to enter a blocked wall cell.

Returning to the hallway
Provide a Return to hallway action inside content rooms.

When returning:

Restore the same incoming hallway.
Place the knight safely just below its destination threshold, facing down.
Require the knight to leave the threshold before that entrance can activate again.
Do not reopen the content room immediately in a loop.
Previous-room entrance
Recommended implementation: use the arrival-side threshold to reopen the room the visitor came from.

For consistency, the arrival threshold can occupy the two cells directly below the spawn:

(spawn X, spawn Y + 1)
(spawn X + 1, spawn Y + 1)
These positions are walkable in all seven layouts.

This is a recommended backward-navigation behavior; the existing collision guides specifically document the forward destination triggers.

Map backgrounds

Use the existing 0.5× map export setting for 384 × 288 output.
For the simplest first version, use the complete environment image as the background, then draw the knight separately above it.

The tile atlas supports a later tile-by-tile reconstruction. That reconstruction is not required to make the first version work.

Pixel scaling
Use nearest-neighbor scaling.

Prefer integer display scales:

1×: 384 × 288
2×: 768 × 576
3×: 1152 × 864
Movement and collision always use native world coordinates, regardless of display size.

11. Responsive behavior, accessibility, and loading
Desktop
Show the entire hallway and its destination label.
Keep the D-pad available even when keyboard controls are supported.
Allow content rooms to scroll normally.
Mobile
Stack the map and controls when necessary.
Use the largest integer map scale that fits.
Make touch targets larger than the visual pixel-arrow artwork.
Do not let D-pad presses accidentally scroll the page.
Accessibility
Give directional controls clear accessible names.
Preserve visible keyboard focus.
Do not communicate status through color alone.
Respect reduced-motion preferences.
Provide textual location and destination guidance.
A useful accessibility option is stepwise movement buttons that move the character through the same valid cells. They should still require doorway entry rather than skip directly to a chapter.

Loading
Load required map and character assets before enabling movement.

If something is unavailable, show a clear retry state. Do not let the knight move through an invisible environment.

 Keyboard and D-pad movement both work.
 Walls, props, and map boundaries block movement correctly.
 Every spawn has a valid route to its destination.
 A hallway’s forward room opens only through its threshold.
 Holding a direction cannot trigger multiple room openings.
 Returning from a room does not cause an immediate reopening loop.
 No direct “Next room” shortcut bypasses exploration.
 Room progress and wellness progress remain independent.

 Pixel assets stay crisp and correctly scaled.

The central rule: the scrapbook remains the main experience; the dungeon provides short, consistent, meaningful transitions between its chapters.
